Project 2: If it is broke, fix it
Why redesign? 
A fundamental redesign is not a tweak around the edges, like improving the layout of one menu or changing from a light to a dark color scheme.   What I mean by a fundamental redesign is a significant change to address a significant issue or to reach a significantly different goal. 

There are a variety of reasons that someone may want to take an existing UI and undertake a fundamental redesign.  I think these reasons likely fall into 2 major categories: 

A) The current UI is not usable (learnable, efficient to operate and with few and reversible errors) or has significant design flaws (too complex, poor use of design principles- layout, color, text hierarchies, ....).   This likely includes many important interfaces that do not receive much UI design and development support-  UI's made by non-profits or small businesses, university websites.  

B) The current UI is usable, but it has a design goal that has some core flaw.   Perhaps it aims to achieve one set of goals, at the expense of others.  Consider a social media site that prioritizes ads, endless scrolling, and toxic engagement, over connection with your friends and family.  Consider a music streaming site that pushes algorithmic streams so aggressively, that you can't make playlists or cultivate your own tastes anymore.  Or perhaps the UI is usable for many people, but not for a certain group of people (children, the elderly, patients in a hospital). 

C) Perhaps you think a UI would be more effective and interesting if it followed a different design strategy.   Perhaps it should use principles in motivation and gameification.  What if email management was game-ified?  Would more students do their assignments, if if Canvas was structured like an interactive video game?  Maybe the current standard approach, is ineffective for certain tasks:  Are digital photo albums the best way to display photos of vacations, where you go to different locations?  What if the digital wall in Langsam focused on interactions from phones or tablets, not laptops?  How would a news website have to change if it wanted to display the news for a large display in a public area rather than a personal display on a small device?

2) Redesign research, data, planning:  Follow a process for planning your redesign, and gather research+data.  

A good redesign process will proceed from a solid motivation, draw from research or data, and be specific in diagnosing the problems.  Since everyone's project is different, you need to consider what research you need to do or data you need to gather to justify and ground your redesign.

Implementation 
Implement some meaningful portion of this envisioned redesign in a working prototype, written using Svelte or SvelteKit and Javascript.  This prototype should be hosted publicly, so we can test it.  

As with other projects- think of this as a prototype to illustrate the new design.  Select the most interesting aspects of your redesign- no need to spend time on login pages or a routine settings page if this is not illustrating core goals in your redesign.  By "a meaningful portion" I could envision a main page and a subpage or two.  This depends on the UI you select and the major issues you want to tackle.  

As with other projects- there is no need for a backend or a database.  I tend to think this takes time away from front-end work and introduces un-needed complexity to projects in this class.  This time, however, I won't penalize you if you want to do a backend with a database.  Just make sure you can publicly host your code for us to test.   Do this at your own risk, because it does take time away from the front end work.
